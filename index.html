About this data

Methodology & sources

Source
Daily stock prices from Yahoo Finance (via the yfinance library), 2019–01–01 through 2025–12–31. The list of companies and their GICS sub-industry comes from Wikipedia's "List of S&P 500 companies," filtered to the current Energy sector (21 companies).
What one row is
One row is one S&P 500 Energy-sector company in one calendar year. Each row's price and volume figures are computed from that company's daily closes and volumes during that year.
Which rows are dropped, and why
145 of a possible 147 rows (21 companies × 7 years) are present. EXE (Expand Energy) is missing 2019 and 2020: it is the post–Chapter 11 successor ticker to Chesapeake Energy, which only began trading again in February 2021, so no legitimate price exists for it before then.
A limitation to know about
This uses today's S&P 500 Energy lineup, not the lineup as it stood in any single past year. A few energy companies that were in the S&P 500 earlier in 2019–2025 but have since been acquired or merged out — including Hess (acquired by Chevron) and Marathon Oil (acquired by ConocoPhillips) — are not included, since they are no longer current constituents. This slightly favors companies that survived as independent acquirers rather than targets.
How each summary number is computed
Companies tracked is the count of distinct tickers in the file. Average annual return is the mean of annual_return_pct across all 145 company-year rows. Best cumulative return is, for each company, its last available year's close price divided by its first available year's open price, minus one; the highest value across all 21 companies is shown. Average annual volatility is the mean of annual_volatility_pct (the annualized standard deviation of that company's daily returns within the year) across all 145 rows.
